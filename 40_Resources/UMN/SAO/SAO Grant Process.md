# Google doc Information
Content drafted for four Google Docs, summarizing the 2026-2027 SAO Grants Training Course exactly as taught, for any group reading it before it has a specific event picked out. Source: every note in the Training Course folder. Nothing here is customized to a specific event or cycle - the group's actual event, budget, and cycle dates get filled in once those are decided (check the SAO Grant Deadlines resource for current cycle dates, since deadlines change per cycle).
Each doc ends with its own Resources section, split from `Training Course/Resources.md`. Within each section: **Key Points** bullet the eligibility rules and other facts everyone needs to know cold, paragraphs carry the supporting detail, and **Action Items** are the generic next steps any group should take at that stage.
## First Doc - Event Planning & Logistics
### Why This Doc Exists
Before a proposal can be written, the event itself needs to be planned in enough detail to describe, budget, and defend. This doc walks through that planning in the order it actually needs to happen: decide the basics, build the budget, lock the venue, clear permits, plan for accessibility, plan promotion, and set up how the group debriefs afterward. Everything here feeds directly into the proposal questions in the Second Doc.
### The Three Decisions to Make First
There are three decisions every group needs to make before starting any other logistics planning, because each one shapes the choices that come after it.
**Key Points:**
- Total budget available - the ceiling the group is comfortable spending, decided before looking at grant funding
- Date or date range - work backward from this to build a task list and deadlines
- Goals of the event - a specific, defined outcome, not a vague statement
If applying for an SAO Grant, the date decision should happen at least 3 to 4 months before the event to meet application deadlines. Goals matter beyond motivation - they give the group a way to judge afterward whether the event actually worked, and they're required later in the proposal.
**Action Items:**
- Set a total budget ceiling before discussing specific costs
- Pick a date or date range that leaves 3 to 4 months before a grant application deadline
- Write the event's goals as specific, measurable outcomes
### Building the Budget
A budget is a planning tool, not just a spending cap.
**Key Points:**
- A well-built budget identifies what resources the event actually needs
- It improves decision-making by giving someone clear spending authority and accountability
- It lets the group monitor its financial position throughout planning
- It gives the group real data to debrief with afterward
The recommended process: break anticipated costs into categories (venue, food, supplies, promotional materials, speakers, etc.), research actual costs for each category and pick the combination that meets the event's goals without breaking the budget, execute while tracking spending as it happens, and revise as real numbers come in. Build a budget sheet with individual line items so the group can explain exactly how funds are being used, keep every receipt (photograph and file them in a shared folder), and maintain a general ledger tracking all income and expenses in one place for an ongoing picture of financial health.
**Action Items:**
- Build a line-item budget sheet organized by category
- Photograph and file every receipt in a shared folder as purchases happen
- Set up a general ledger (the Ledger Template linked below is a starting point)
### Choosing and Booking a Venue
Before reserving anything, define what the event actually needs.
**Key Points:**
- Atmosphere, room size, AV/tech needs, and venue budget should all be defined before searching
- A Major Event (large crowds, media, or high-profile speakers/performers) may require enhanced security or emergency personnel and additional University review
- Large venue spaces should be reserved at least two months out; some large venues take a semester to a year of lead time
- It is recommended that groups do not book a venue until grant funding has been awarded, since a denied or forfeited grant can still leave the group responsible for booking fees
Conference and Event Services is the starting point for most reservable campus spaces. Verify availability early, and review the venue's cancellation policy in advance in case the group can no longer move forward with the event. Review the Disability Resource Center's accessibility guidelines when picking and setting up a venue, so the space works for every attendee from the start.
**Action Items:**
- List venue needs (atmosphere, size, AV/tech, budget) before searching for a space
- Confirm whether the event meets the Major Event threshold and, if so, loop in the Student Activities Office for the review process
- Reserve large venues at least two months ahead, and read the cancellation policy first
- Avoid booking a venue before grant funding is awarded, if funding is being requested
### Permits, Permissions, and Insurance
Some events need extra approvals, and deadlines vary widely, so they need to be checked early.
**Key Points:**
- Serving food (pizza, potlucks, bake sales, catering) requires a food permit at least ten business days (two weeks) in advance
- Fundraisers and sales require a review against fundraising policy and the group's classification
- Alcohol requires an Alcohol Use Application at least fifteen business days (three weeks) in advance
- Public performance of films, TV, or video games requires a public performance license at least fourteen days in advance
- Concerts or dances require direct contact with the Student Activities Office at least one month in advance to discuss security fees and policy
- General liability insurance may be required; if the group has no coverage of its own, a Tenant User's Liability Insurance Policy (TULIP) can be purchased through Tulip Insurance
- Events involving minors on campus (without an accompanying adult, or with an overnight stay) require everyone with ongoing interaction with those minors to complete required training and a background check under the Regents Policy on the Safety of Minors
If the group is already working on or has secured a permit, that should be stated in the proposal - it's useful context for the advisor reviewing the application.
**Action Items:**
- Identify every permit the event needs and file each one by its specific deadline
- Determine whether general liability insurance is needed, and pursue TULIP coverage if the group has none
- If minors will attend without an accompanying adult, confirm required training and background checks are complete before the event
### Accessibility
Accessibility planning should start as early as the event format and venue decisions, not get added afterward.
**Key Points:**
- It is recommended that all groups appoint an Access Coordinator
- The Access Coordinator receives, confirms, and tracks all accessibility and accommodation requests, including food accommodations (halal, vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free)
- Venue accessibility (elevators, ramps, accessible pathways and seating, restrooms) should be researched before confirming a space
- Accessible marketing includes a registration question about accommodations
Where possible, involve people who identify with disabilities in the planning itself, so the group anticipates needs it might otherwise miss. On the day of the event: post directional signage, staff entry points and elevators, use an inclusive check-in process, brief all staff on the accessibility plan in advance, keep pathways clear for wheelchair access, and have photographers ask permission and avoid flash.
**Action Items:**
- Name an Access Coordinator and list them as the contact for accommodation requests in event promotion
- Add an accommodation question (dietary and disability-related) to event registration
- Research the venue's accessibility features before confirming it
- Brief all event staff on the accessibility plan before doors open
### Promotion
Promotion needs to start well before the event.
**Key Points:**
- Before designing anything, define the audience, the timeline, and what makes the event worth someone's time
- On-campus channels: posters/flyers, chalking (must follow the University's chalking policy), and GopherLink
- Online channels: Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X, and Snapchat each have different strengths
- Consistency, engagement, a clear call to action, and checking analytics all improve results
Posters and flyers work best with short text, 2 to 3 fonts, and a QR code or link to the event page. GopherLink is the best free tool for listing event details and tracking outreach. Online, Instagram works well with Stories/Reels and hashtags, Facebook with event pages and groups, TikTok with behind-the-scenes content, X with real-time engagement, and Snapchat with Geofilters.
**Action Items:**
- Define the target audience and promotional timeline before creating materials
- List the event on GopherLink with full details
- Pick 2 to 3 online channels that fit the audience and post consistently
- Include a clear call to action in every piece of promotion
### After the Event: Debrief and Documentation
Post-event reflection is not optional cleanup - it directly shapes future funding and planning.
**Key Points:**
- Collect feedback within 24 to 48 hours of the event
- A short attendee survey (5 to 10 questions) and a team debrief are the two main feedback tools
- Review budget vs. actuals, promotion channel performance, venue/logistics, and accessibility gaps
- Document findings in an event summary and update the event tracker with real numbers
Frame the team debrief around learning rather than blame. Write a short event summary (attendance, final budget, key feedback, what worked, what to improve) and store it somewhere future officers can find it, then hand this off explicitly during officer transitions.
**Action Items:**
- Send an attendee survey within 24 to 48 hours of the event
- Hold a team debrief shortly after the event
- Update the event tracker and general ledger with final attendance and cost figures
- Write an event summary and store it for future officers

## Second Doc - Pre-Application & Writing the Proposal
### How the SAO Grants Program Works
SAO Grants is run through the Student Activities Office to help registered student groups fund special events.
**Key Points:**
- Pre-Application - eligibility, event planning, and allowable expenses; this is where most of a group's actual work happens
- Application - submitting a complete, eligible proposal
- Pre-Event - requirements before hosting the awarded event
- Post-Event - confirming the funding was used correctly
This doc covers the first two stages: Pre-Application and Application.
### Determining Eligibility: Three Areas
Before drafting anything, the group needs to confirm three separate things are eligible, because failing any one of them kills the whole application regardless of how good the event idea is.
**Key Points:**
- Applicant eligibility - is the group and are its applicants in good standing?
- Event eligibility - does the specific event qualify?
- Expense eligibility - do the specific costs being requested qualify?
### Group and Applicant Eligibility
**Key Points:**
- A group in good standing on GopherLink shows no label before its group type (RSO or CLP); "Probation" or "Expired" labels make a group ineligible
- Groups must be currently registered as an RSO or CLP at the time of application
- A group that has submitted its annual registration but isn't fully processed can still apply, but must be fully registered before its first funded event date or September 30, 2026, whichever comes first - missing that deadline forfeits the grant
- Both the primary and secondary applicants must be listed as officers of the group on GopherLink
- Both applicants must complete the SAO Grants Training Course with an overall grade of 90% or better, regardless of past grant experience
The SAO Grants Team checks all of this before the application even reaches the committee. If the group or applicants aren't eligible, the application is denied immediately and never reviewed by the committee.
**Action Items:**
- Check the group's GopherLink status before applying (not on probation, not expired)
- Confirm both the primary and secondary applicants are listed officers on GopherLink
- Have both applicants complete this training course at 90% or better before applying
### Event and Expense Eligibility
A handful of event characteristics cause immediate denial, regardless of anything else in the application.
**Key Points (automatic denial if the event):**
- Includes, or is hosted somewhere that offers, alcohol
- Includes gambling, including raffles (a raffle requires payment to enter; free entry makes it a "prize drawing" instead, which is treated differently)
- Is a meeting, rehearsal, practice, or other operational gathering
- Is being hosted for recruitment or fundraising purposes
- Violates University Policy
For expenses, the group needs to be ready to answer, per item: is it being rented or bought, how and when will it be used during the event (tied to the event's schedule), is it single-use (reusable items may be treated as operational inventory instead of event expenses), and how did the group land on this cost estimate. An exact-to-the-penny number isn't required, but the reasoning needs to be shown, and unused funds get returned during the post-event evaluation.
**Key Points (commonly ineligible expenses):**
- Events held as fundraisers to generate revenue for the group or an outside organization
- Reusable items that go into the group's inventory
- Prizes or giveaways (gift cards, thank-you gifts, participation prizes)
- Events where alcohol is available
- Stipends or honoraria for any University of Minnesota student, faculty, or staff member
- Weekly or biweekly meetings, practices, rehearsals, or officer training
- General operating costs and group supplies
- Events solely intended for recruitment
- Partisan political organizations
**Action Items:**
- Cross-check the planned event against the automatic-denial list before drafting a proposal
- Cross-check every planned expense against the ineligible-expense list before requesting funding for it
- For every requested expense, document whether it's rented or bought, when it's used, and how the cost was estimated
### RSO Requirement: Keep the W-9 Current
If a group is a Registered Student Organization, it needs a current IRS W-9 on file to avoid funding delays.
**Key Points:**
- Enter the group's legal name (and any "doing business as" name)
- Check the correct federal tax classification (most groups check "Other" and specify "unincorporated association"; nonprofit corporations specify differently depending on tax-exempt status)
- Enter a mailing address for tax correspondence and the group's EIN from Form SS-4
- Sign and date the form
**Action Items:**
- RSOs should confirm their W-9 is current before applying for a grant
### Writing the Application: Overview
Once group, event, and expense eligibility are confirmed, the group completes one proposal per event: an event proposal and a budget proposal, both reviewed together by the SAO Grants Committee.
**Key Points:**
- The proposal template has two required tabs - the Event Description and Justification tab, and the SAO Grant Budget Template tab - both must be complete for the application to be considered complete
- The committee uses viewpoint neutrality when reviewing applications - it does not infer anything that isn't explicitly written down
Since nothing is inferred, if something matters to understanding the event, it has to be spelled out directly in the application itself.
### The Nine Proposal Questions
**Question 1 - Describe the event in detail.** Cover date, location, attendance, purpose/desired outcome, and specifically how this differs from the group's normal operational gatherings. If attending a conference, explain how it connects to educational development. Venue and date can be changed later with Grants Team approval, but moving an event off campus reduces eligible funding and may require reimbursement.
*Action item:* Write out explicitly how the event differs from a normal group gathering - don't rely on the committee to infer this.
**Question 2 - Budgeted expenses and income.** List every expense with a justified amount, what budget line it falls under, why it's necessary, and whether it's being rented or purchased. Include research behind the requested amount, and build in a buffer for price changes as part of each line item's estimate, not as a separate line. This must match the Budget Proposal tab exactly.
*Action item:* Research each expense, build a buffer into the estimate itself, and confirm every number matches the Budget Proposal tab.
**Question 3 - How does this event align with the group's mission and goals?** Include the group's actual mission statement, matching what's listed on GopherLink.
*Action item:* Pull the group's mission statement directly from its governing documents and GopherLink profile before writing this answer.
**Question 4 - Detailed event agenda.** Times, locations, and activities for the whole event, including when each requested item from Question 2 actually gets used, and how much setup/teardown time is needed.
*Action item:* Map every requested expense from Question 2 to the specific time in the agenda it gets used.
**Question 5 - How does the event promote student development, enhance diversity/equity/inclusion, and/or benefit the campus community?** Not every event needs to hit all three - pick the one that fits best and address it directly.
*Action item:* Choose the single impact area that fits the event best and write a direct, specific answer rather than a generic one.
**Question 6 - Describe the planning process in detail.** What's already confirmed or booked, what's still on the to-do list, and (if partnering with another group) how the planning is being split.
*Action item:* List what's confirmed/booked and what's still outstanding as of the application date.
**Question 7 - Promotional and publicity plan.** If claiming "for all students" eligibility, be specific about how any student would be welcome and made aware of the event - this directly determines which SAO Grant Initiatives (funding sources) the group qualifies for.
*Action item:* If claiming "for all students," detail specific outreach steps reaching multiple student groups, not just the applicant's own membership.
**Question 8 - For partnership grants only.** Describe how the event is being planned across all partnering groups and each group's specific responsibilities. If the group isn't partnering, answer "N/A."
*Action item:* If the event is a partnership, this question must be answered in detail - leaving it blank results in automatic denial as an incomplete application.
**Question 9 - Student Event and Entertainment (SEE) Initiative only, optional.** Requires explaining the cultural/subject-matter knowledge needed to run the event respectfully, and demonstrating a predominantly UMN Twin Cities audience. This initiative requires at least 200 attendees and cannot fall on blackout dates.
*Action item:* Only answer this question if applying for the SEE Initiative and the event meets the 200-attendee minimum; otherwise skip it.
### The Budget Proposal Tab
**Key Points:**
- The budget proposal must include ALL potential income and ALL expenses for the event, not just what's being requested from SAO
- It must include all group contributions, other funding sources, and any ticket sales
- The budget must be balanced - the template shows a green confirmation when total expenses match total income
- An unbalanced budget is denied immediately, before the committee sees it
- Every expense listed here must match Question 2 exactly, including costs the SAO Grant won't be covering
**Action Items:**
- Build the budget proposal with every expense and every income source, not just the funding request
- Confirm the budget balances before submitting
- Cross-check every line against Question 2 for consistency

## Third Doc - Before You Host Your Event
### Where to Find Requirements and Deadlines
**Key Points:**
- Every pre-event and post-event requirement and deadline is listed in the award notification email
- Requirements can differ from group to group depending on committee and advisor review
Since requirements aren't standardized across every group, the award notification has to be read in full and referenced throughout the process rather than assumed from a general webpage.
**Action Items:**
- Read the full award notification as soon as it arrives and keep it accessible throughout planning
### If the Application Is Denied
**Key Points:**
- There is no appeals process for SAO Grants - committee decisions are final
- The recommended path is to reapply in the next funding cycle if the event still fits those deadlines
**Action Items:**
- If denied, request feedback from the SAO Grants Team or the Student Group Services Advisor before reapplying
### If the Application Is Awarded
**Key Points:**
- Committee decisions go out via email about two weeks after the application deadline, one week after the committee meeting
- Within two weeks of the award message, the group must submit a Statement of Agreement
- There are separate forms for RSOs and CLPs - the correct one must be signed and submitted
- Missing the two-week window forfeits the grant
- Some groups must also schedule a meeting with their Student Activities Advisor, listed under "Pre-Event Requirements" in the award notification (new applicants and large-scale events are typically required to meet)
**Action Items:**
- Submit the correct Statement of Agreement (RSO or CLP) within two weeks of the award message
- Check the award notification for whether an advisor meeting is required and schedule it promptly
### Receiving Funding and Preparing for the Event
**Key Points:**
- Funding typically arrives within six weeks of completing the pre-event requirements in the award notification
- Every receipt for event expenses must be kept, matching the Acceptable Forms of Documentation guidelines
- All event promotions must include SAO Grant logos (and Coke logos too, if funded through a Coke initiative such as Coke Activity, Coke Development, or SEE)
- Events should use Coca-Cola products; alternate beverages require prior approval from saogrant@umn.edu
Failing to keep proper receipts can require partially reimbursing the grant after the event. If a vendor won't provide proper documentation, that issue should be flagged to the SAO Grants Team immediately, not after the fact.
**Action Items:**
- Keep every receipt from the moment purchasing starts, matching the documentation guidelines
- Add SAO Grant logos (and Coke logos if applicable) to every piece of event promotion
- Request beverage exceptions in advance if not using Coca-Cola products
- Reach out to saogrant@umn.edu for funding status updates if six weeks pass without funding
### Paying Individuals for Services
**Key Points:**
- Payments to individuals need reasonable justification and must be authorized by the group's constitution and by-laws
- All payments (salaries, wages, stipends, honoraria) are taxable by the IRS and Minnesota
- Groups with payroll must withhold FICA, federal income tax, and Minnesota income tax, and must pay employer FICA, Minnesota unemployment tax, and workers' compensation
- Independent contractors paid $600 or more in a calendar year must receive a Form 1099-MISC by the following January 31 (this applies to officer stipends too)
- A copy of the 1099-MISC and Form 1096 must go to the IRS by the end of February, with a copy of the 1099-MISC also going to the Minnesota Department of Revenue
Authorization should ideally be documented in writing in meeting minutes, with a contract drawn up for the individual being paid.
**Action Items:**
- Confirm any individual payment is authorized in the group's constitution/by-laws before paying
- Track cumulative payments to each individual across the year - $600 or more triggers a 1099-MISC requirement

## Fourth Doc - After Your Event & FAQs
### The Post-Event Process
**Key Points:**
- The Post-Event Evaluation Form is due within 30 days of the event
- All SAO Grant funding stays tentative until this review is complete - a grant can still be forfeited for missing this deadline even after a successful event
- The form requires: copies of every event expense receipt, a completed and balanced Post-Event Budget, and a description of the event (attendance, outcome, what was learned)
- Missing the 30-day deadline forfeits the grant and requires returning all SAO Grant funding
The Post-Event Budget is the final sheet of the proposal and should cover all expenses and income for the event. It should be balanced too - the only exception is if the group was overfunded, or had to supplement with its own money, which is logged under a "group contribution" line item. If the event received additional funding beyond the SAO Grant, documentation of that income needs to be included as well. The event description is submitted through the Asana form.
**Action Items:**
- Assign an owner for the post-event evaluation form as soon as the event ends
- Gather receipts, build the post-event budget, and write the event description within 30 days of the event
- Submit on time even if some documentation is still outstanding (see the FAQ below)
### Frequently Asked Questions
**Key Points:**
- Receipts must include: date of transaction, vendor name, itemized list and quantity of items, price of each item, total cost, and proof of purchase; screenshots of Venmo, Cash App, PayPal, or bank transactions are not accepted
- Event dates can move, but not past May 31, 2027, and not outside the eligible dates for the cycle applied under; an event change request form must be submitted before the current event date on file
- Recruitment events are not eligible for funding
- Food at a regular meeting is not eligible for funding, since meetings are operational events
- There is no appeals process; committee decisions are final
The recommended workaround for the lack of an appeals process is applying two cycles ahead of time, so there's room to reapply using the committee's feedback if the first attempt doesn't succeed. If documentation is missing at the 30-day deadline, best practice is to still submit on time with a note listing which receipts are outstanding and why, and to loop in the Grants Team or Student Group Services Advisor for help - not to wait past the deadline.
**Action Items:**
- Use only itemized receipts or paid invoices - never payment-app screenshots
- Submit an event change request before moving an event date, and confirm the new date is still within the eligible range
- Do not apply for funding for recruitment-focused events or regular meeting costs
- If planning to reapply after a denial, consider applying two cycles ahead of time
