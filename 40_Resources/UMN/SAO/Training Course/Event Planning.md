# Event Planning
## One-Line Answer
Lock in ==total budget, event date, and event goals== before touching logistics - these three decisions drive every downstream planning choice.
# Event Planning: How to Get Started
## Event Planning Brainstorm:
Brainstorming is typically one of the first steps involved in the event planning process. In this phase, your group will start generating ideas of what you want your event to be. These ideas can be themes, performers, speakers, or all of the above. It is easy to go straight from the initial brainstorm to starting to determine logistics. However, there are some important steps before you get started that will help your group be set up for success.

---
There are **3 main decisions** that your group will need to make before starting the rest of your logistics planning.
- Total Budget Available
- Date of your event
- Goals of your event

---
### Total Budget Available
- The amount of money your group is able to spend on your event is going to determine a lot of your other decisions while planning. Being up front about the total amount your group is able to spend prior to planning the details of your event will ensure that you stay within an amount you are comfortable with.
### Date of Your Event
- Determining the date of your event, or at least a timeframe you would like to host your event, is going to help your group with other event planning decisions. Aspects of your event such as venue options, funding options, and promotion opportunities are all impacted by how far out from your event you are planning.
    - Start with event date and work backwards
    - Create timeline/task list based on your events needs and deadlines
- If you are considering applying for SAO Grants, this timeline should be at least 3-4 months prior to your event date in order to meet [deadlinesLinks to an external site.](https://z.umn.edu/saogranteligibility).
### Goals of Your Event
- Setting goals is a critical component of planning your events and should be done at the beginning stages of the event planning process. Setting goals gives your group a direction and a vision as you enter the planning stages since it helps turn your group’s initial brainstorming into something defined and actively achievable. It can also help in the overall evaluation of your event and determining whether or not it was successful.

### [Example Grant:Links to an external site.](https://z.umn.edu/saoexamplegrant "Link")
**Brainstorm**: The Gopher Cooking Club #9999 had the idea to host a taco night with homemade tacos and free food for everyone. They want to hire a private chef and have a free cooking class for club members to learn how to cook tacos. They also want this to be an opportunity to supply free food to students on campus. 
**Total Budget Available**: The Gopher Cooking Club would like to spend as little group funding as possible in order to maintain a healthy budget for other initiatives. To do that, and still host their event, they look at [funding opportunitiesLinks to an external site.](https://z.umn.edu/sgfunding) for the group. This includes SAO Grants. Based on SAO Grant's Eligibility Table, the group decides to budget around $2500 for their event, which they believe is enough to host their event on campus. 
**Date of the Event**: The Gopher Cooking Club would like to host this event in April. They believe will allow them enough time to plan the event and apply for funding since April is currently 5 months away. 
**Goals of the Event**:
- Offer free food to 150 students on campus.
- Create an opportunity for group members to learn from a professional chef both how to cook Tacos and professional advice.
