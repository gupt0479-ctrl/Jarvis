## Phase 9 — Ingestion Latency: The Google Case (2026-07-19 → 2026-07-23)
The user noticed a real Google SWE internship never reached the vault and pushed back on the "closed" framing — a fair challenge that reopened Priority 2. Investigation, in order:
1. Direct live check of SimplifyJobs' feed (this session, via `gh api`/`curl`): Google has 8 entries, not zero — ruling out "Google isn't covered."
2. Of the 2 entries matching term/category/location, one is Master's-only and disqualified by `degrees_allow: ["Bachelor's"]` regardless of timing — a premise correction the other session caught on its own pass.
3. The one real candidate (`820400f1...`, Bachelor's, Summer 2027, Software, US) is `active: false`. The other session found every one of Google's 8 entries — active and inactive alike — has `date_posted`/`date_updated` roughly 60-120 seconds apart, meaning SimplifyJobs' own bot resolves a Google record almost immediately after ingesting it. The real active window was on the order of a minute or two — smaller than any polling interval this project would reasonably run.
4. **Verdict: not our latency, not catchable by tighter cadence.** A general architectural risk was named separately and correctly: the write-time `active`-gate silently prevents ever writing a posting that flips true→false *between* two of our polls, for postings with a real few-hour-to-a-day window (unlike Google's case). A "write on first sight regardless of active status" fix was proposed (option B) and explicitly **deferred, reframed as a future log-only diagnostic, not a dossier-writer** — a dead-on-arrival dossier costs a review cycle without ever producing an application, which cuts against this project's own stated success metric.

## Phase 13 — OPT Regex, Measured And Improved Against Real Data (2026-07-25)
Every one of zshah101's 22 real `citizens-only`-tagged postings was Firecrawl-fetched and checked against the live `OPT_EXCLUSION_RE`, as a real test set rather than a guess at new phrasing:
- **Baseline: 6 of 22 (27%) caught.**
- Real misses showed a second common phrasing the original 6 patterns never covered — ITAR/export-control language ("requires access to export-controlled information... 'U.S. Person' status," "must... be a 'U.S. person' as defined by 22 C.F.R. § 120.62") at Saronic, Hermeus, Varda Space — indirect/definitional, not a direct imperative.
- **One new pattern added**, built from that real text, validated against every existing non-signal fixture first (Palantir's conditional background-investigation line, Grant Thornton's EEO boilerplate, sponsorship-question cases) — zero new false positives.
- **Result: 13 of 22 (59%) caught**, re-measured on the same 22 postings, shipped with a regression test.
- **Remaining 9 misses, categorized honestly rather than treated as one problem:** 1 looks like a tagging false positive on zshah101's side (the posting's real text is visa-*permissive*). 1 (Rocket Lab) has no citizenship language on the posting itself — the keyword hit was in an unrelated sidebar listing, likely a company-level mistagging. 2 (both Palantir entries) use the same conditional-willingness language already ruled non-exclusionary in Phase 6 — correct behavior, not a miss. 5 (CACI ×2, Northrop Grumman, Torch Technologies, one Motorola posting) show **no citizenship signal anywhere in the fetched page text** — very likely because those ATS platforms (mostly Workday) ask citizenship as an interactive application-form question, structurally invisible to a page-content scrape. No regex change fixes that.
**Verified 2026-07-25 (fresh session):** `ingestion/posting_page.py`'s `OPT_EXCLUSION_RE`, as committed in `a21b2fa`, does carry the two new export-control alternations described above (`export.control.{0,150}u\.?s\.?\s*person` and the reverse order, `re.I | re.S`). Confirmed against the actual shipped regression test in `tests/test_posting_page.py` (`test_explicit_negative_signals_exclude`, 15/15 passing in this file) using the real Saronic/Hermeus/Varda Space text quoted there — not re-derived from imagination. The full 22-posting Firecrawl sweep was not re-run this session (that's a live-network re-measurement, not a static check); the regex pattern and its regression coverage are what were verified.

## Backlog Decision And Ship (2026-07-25)
The four-source addition (Greenhouse, Ashby, `vanshb03`, `zshah101`) produced a one-time backlog of 186 new-uid candidates in a live dry run, 171 of which would clear the write gate on the very first run — a spike against the Phase 6 sizing assumption of <100 Firecrawl fetches/month. Recommendation given: throttle rather than discard the backlog or absorb it all at once — discarding loses real, currently-open postings that are the entire reason these sources were added; absorbing all 171 at once would dump new dossiers on a promotion queue already sitting at zero. **This session's `gh api` check confirms the batch is live**: commit `a21b2fa` ("Four new discovery sources: vanshb03, zshah101, Greenhouse, Ashby"), pushed 2026-07-25T16:45:34Z.
**Verified 2026-07-25 (fresh session, direct repo access):** it's a genuine throttle, not the absorb-or-discard options that were merely discussed. `run_pipeline.py:64` sets `MAX_NEW_WRITES_PER_RUN = 18`; `_prioritize_and_cap()` (`run_pipeline.py:67-76`) sorts all new candidates by `date_posted` descending — most-recently-posted first, an item with no `date_posted` sorts last, never first — and returns the top 18 as `this_run`, the remainder as `deferred`. Deferred items are simply never passed to `validate_and_write`, so they're never marked seen and `dedup_new()` naturally re-offers them on the next run — no extra state to track the backlog separately. Wired into `run_once()` (`run_pipeline.py:261`), which also now logs a `deferred_count` field per run.
**This isn't just code review — it's confirmed running in production.** `logs/runs.jsonl`'s most recent entry (2026-07-25T16:52:23Z, the first scheduled `run.yml` execution after `a21b2fa` was pushed) shows `fetch_counts` for all 6 sources, `written_count: 13`, `deferred_count: 166` — the throttle firing for real against a real backlog, not a hypothetical. (`written` came in under the 18 cap because some of the 18 highest-priority candidates still failed other write-gate checks, e.g. `cross_source_duplicate` or `url_liveness` — expected, not a bug.)
