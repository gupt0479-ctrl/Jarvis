# Background — The Vision Behind the Desk
==This is not a broker, not a signal service, not TradingView.com. It is a private research desk for one person — a place where a brain built from real math watches fourteen stocks, argues with itself about what it sees, proves its ideas against history before it trusts them, and keeps a paper trading journal so its owner can learn to trade by watching the brain learn to trade.==
## What this actually is
Anant is building `research_data` — internally still called **TradingView**, a name borrowed for now, not owned, not affiliated with the real company. The product has no customers. It has one operator, hardcoded into the system as `anant`, and every architectural decision in this project bends toward that fact: no auth screens, no multi-tenant anything, no onboarding flow, no pricing page. It is closer to a cockpit than a website — one seat, built to fit one person exactly, built to be lived in for hours at a time, every day, for years.
The desk watches a fixed universe of fourteen symbols — `VOO VTI SPY QQQ AAPL MSFT NVDA AMZN GOOGL META BRK.B JPM COST TSLA` — no more, no fewer, until real evidence earns the universe a fifteenth. Underneath the UI this document plans, a Python and DuckDB backend already exists: it pulls prices from Polygon, Tiingo, and FMP, computes deterministic factor math (momentum, quality, safety, valuation, all benchmarked against VOO), and feeds those numbers — never raw prices — to two small language models playing two very different roles.
## The brain, in the order it actually thinks
1. **The data layer** ingests OHLCV and fundamentals, grades every record on a strict quality ladder — `MISSING > CONTRADICTORY > STALE > INSUFFICIENT_DATA > PARTIAL > USABLE` — and refuses to let anything downstream pretend a record is better than it is.
2. **The factor engine** turns clean data into a `ScorePacket`: a momentum rank (12-1 month return, Jegadeesh–Titman), a safety rank (inverse realized volatility), a quality score (FCF/EV-weighted), a valuation read, and descriptive technical context (MA20/50/200, RSI-14, Bollinger, ATR) that is never allowed to drive a decision on its own. Every symbol gets ranked 1 through 14 against its thirteen neighbors, not scored in a vacuum.
3. **The Analyst** reads only the `ScorePacket`, never a raw price, and writes a structured **EvidenceCard**: an action, a confidence number, a plain-language case, supporting evidence, risks, opposing evidence, and the exact conditions that would prove the thesis wrong. It is contractually forbidden from writing `BUY`, `SELL`, `guaranteed`, or `risk-free` — the action vocabulary it's allowed is exactly `WATCH | HOLD | ACCUMULATE | REDUCE | AVOID | INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, and every number in its prose must trace back to a real field it was actually shown.
4. **The Critic** reads a narrower slice — four gate-summary numbers, nothing raw — and can only push confidence *down*, never up. It argues for HOLD or demotion when the evidence looks thinner than the Analyst's confidence implies. Two models, two jobs, no third "Trader" agent that gets to pull the trigger — that role was researched (the TradingAgents framework, arXiv 2412.20138) and explicitly rejected, because letting an LLM approve a trade violates the one rule that isn't up for debate.
5. **The four gates** — Out-of-Sample, Monte Carlo, Walk-Forward, Deflated Sharpe — run in that fixed, immutable order on every `StrategySpec` before it's allowed anywhere near paper money. A spec that fails a gate isn't a bug, it's the system working; Phase 2's very first live gate run failed on thin data, and that failure is on record as proof the desk tells the truth about itself before it tells the truth about a stock.
6. **The brain's citation loop** closes on itself: a citation proposes a spec, a human — only a human, `validate_human_identity` hard-blocks `cursor`/`claude`/`fable` from ever approving anything — decides whether to test it, the gates decide whether it's worthy, a promotion or demotion decision gets recorded, and the lesson gets journaled back into the citation table so the next proposal stands on what was actually learned, not on a fresh guess.
7. **Paper trading** is the proof that any of this actually works. A pre-approved `Thesis` enters a position automatically at its computed time — no real money, no broker, ever — and every exit is required to report `voo_return_same_period`, so there is no way to quietly forget the benchmark. Two paced modes exist: an accelerated historical replay that journals as if time had passed, and a live calendar paper book that tracks in real time.
## The philosophy this UI has to carry
Anant said it plainly partway through planning this interface: *"Primarily we are looking to make it look like a game to trade. When to play, when to farm, how to farm, etc. This is going to be a base where I learn trading alongside the brain we are developing... We learn by testing."* That sentence is the actual design brief. Every one of the eight pages this documentation set describes exists to answer one of three questions, on sight, without a manual: **what does the brain think right now, why does it think that, and what happened the last time it thought something like this?** A page that can't answer one of those three questions in under five seconds has failed, no matter how good it looks.
This is also, deliberately, **not** a spreadsheet wearing a dark theme. It's meant to feel like stepping onto the bridge of something — a quiet, confident, slightly cinematic control room where the important thing glows and the unimportant thing recedes. Not loud. Not gamified with confetti and badges for their own sake. The "game" here is closer to a flight simulator than a mobile app: the stakes are real (paper money that tracks real prices, real learning, real decisions about a real future), and the interface earns trust by being honest about uncertainty, not by being flashy.
## The theme: royal blue on void black
Anant's own words: *"Blue and black. The blue is almost like royal blue — kind of a solid, slightly dark blue."* This is the palette every page note in this set is written against, and it should read the same way on every single one of them.
- **The hull** — the base of every page — is not pure black. It's a near-black with a whisper of blue folded into it, the color of a night sky just before you'd call it black, somewhere around `#0A0B12`. Pure black would feel like a terminal someone forgot to theme; this feels like a room with one instrument panel lit in it.
- **The instrumentation** — the brand accent, used for structural chrome: active nav state, focus rings, card borders that matter, the glow around anything currently "alive" — is a **royal blue**, deep and saturated, closer to `#1E3AAE` than to sky blue or the washed-out blue of a corporate SaaS product. It should look expensive and a little serious, like the blue of an old naval uniform or a bank vault door, never like a cheerful app icon.
- **Surfaces** — cards, panels, the sidebar — sit a few shades lighter than the hull, graphite rather than charcoal, so elevation reads without needing a drop shadow to do all the work.
- **Text** is off-white, almost silver, for anything primary; a muted blue-gray for anything secondary (timestamps, captions, "last updated" lines) — never pure white, which would fight the void for attention it doesn't need.
- **The action vocabulary gets its own palette**, deliberately distinct from the royal-blue brand accent so a `HOLD` badge is never mistaken for "this is the important structural blue": WATCH in a cool gray, HOLD in a lighter, more legible blue than the brand royal blue, ACCUMULATE in a confident green, REDUCE in amber, AVOID in a controlled red, INSUFFICIENT_DATA in the same muted gray as WATCH with a dashed border to mark it as absence rather than opinion. Data-quality badges borrow the same green→amber→red logic independently.
- **Motion** is quiet and physical, not bouncy: a soft royal-blue glow breathes on anything currently running (an agent mid-task, a live-refreshing chart); nothing flashes, nothing confetti-pops, nothing celebrates a good trade with a burst animation — the tone is a submarine's control room, not a slot machine.
- **Numbers, tickers, and timestamps** read in a monospace face — that's the one non-negotiable typographic rule, because a desk like this lives and dies on the reader being able to trust that `0.72` really says `0.72` at a glance, not squint-and-guess in a proportional font. Headings can afford a little more character; body prose should stay quiet and legible.
Every page note in `Per Header/` describes exactly how this palette shows up on that specific page — what glows, what pulses, what stays still — so the whole desk reads as one instrument, not eight different apps stapled together.
## Where this sits in the actual build sequence
Nothing about this documentation set skips ahead of where the project actually is. The sequence, in order:
1. **This QNA** — the long clarifying-question exchange between Anant and Cursor (running Haiku 4.5), recorded in full in [[QNA for UI]] — is what locked the shape of all eight pages, the navigation model, the pending-decision propagation rule, and the decision to drop Streamlit for a real coded Next.js/React stack.
2. **Cursor's first pass** at turning that QNA into an engineering spec lives untouched in `Frontend/Codebase/` — thirteen documents, roughly 170 KB, genuinely solid and locked as of 2026-07-29. This documentation set does not edit those files. It exists *alongside* them, going deeper on the parts a pure engineering spec is bad at: the feel, the story, the exact texture of using each page, the theme, the moments the checklist format flattens out.
3. **This set** — [[UI MOC — The Complete Dream]], this background note, and the eight files in `Per Header/` — is the dream fully written out, as if the product already existed and someone were describing it from memory. It is meant for review, for catching what's still wrong or underspecified, before a single line of interface code gets written.
4. **A coded, frontend-only prototype** comes next — real Next.js/React, mock JSON fixtures standing in for the brain, every page and transition built for real so the whole vision can be seen and clicked through, not just read.
5. **Separately and in parallel**, the actual `research_data` backend keeps getting built out — the Python/DuckDB engine this whole UI is a window onto.
6. **Only once both of those are mature** does the real production frontend get built — on the same stack as the prototype, carrying forward whatever code survives contact with reality, with the full cinematic layer (true 3D brain graph, the real agent swarm, every animation this document only describes in words) layered on top.
7. **A `Claude Code/` implementation phase**, planned but not yet started, will turn these vision notes into an actual build plan for Sonnet 5 to execute page by page — slowly, deliberately, with every tiny detail settled first. That is explicitly *not* what this document is; this document is the dream, not the build ticket.
## The rules that don't move
These apply on every page in this set without exception, and any future note that contradicts one of them is the note that's wrong, not the rule:
- **Never BUY or SELL.** The action vocabulary is exactly `WATCH | HOLD | ACCUMULATE | REDUCE | AVOID | INSUFFICIENT_DATA`. Also banned everywhere: `guaranteed`, `risk-free`, and any command-phrased copy ("sell now") — everything reads as a review prompt ("review this because X changed"), never an order.
- **Confidence is always the capped number.** Whatever the Analyst says, whatever the Critic adjusts, the number that ever reaches a screen is the one clamped by that symbol's data-quality ceiling. There is no such thing as an uncapped confidence value anywhere in this UI.
- **Every number traces to something real.** A `ScorePacket` field, a gate metric, a `JournalEntry`. Nothing is UI-invented, nothing is synthesized for effect.
- **The human is the only one who can say yes.** Approving a spec, promoting a strategy, demoting one — those buttons exist, and they only ever fire for `anant`. The UI triggers analysis and ingestion and displays results; it never executes a trade and never approves anything on its own authority.
- **A pending decision is never invisible.** If the brain has decided to enter or exit a position and hasn't yet, that fact shows everywhere the symbol appears — the Dashboard, the Watchlist, both tabs of My Stocks — not just on whichever page happens to be open.
- **The fourteen-symbol universe is fixed.** No ad-hoc ticker search, no "add any stock" box. Growing the universe is a research decision, not a UI feature, until real evidence earns it.
