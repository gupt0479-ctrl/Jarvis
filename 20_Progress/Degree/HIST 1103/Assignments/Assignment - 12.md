Guatemala.  
  
The 1950s were, in my opinion, Guatemala's most volatile decade following World War II. After a coup supported by foreign powers, the nation went from a promising phase of democratic reform to political anarchy throughout these years. A protracted and bloody civil war finally resulted from the changes that started with land reform and ended with military authority.

Jacobo Árbenz was one of the most significant figures during this time. During his presidency, Árbenz sought to modernise Guatemala by giving impoverished farmers access to underused land. Many rural families supported his ideas, but the United Fruit Company and affluent landowners were put in danger. During the Cold War, his reforms also sparked American concerns that Guatemala was headed toward communism.

Carlos Castillo Armas was another important person. He spearheaded the 1954 coup that ousted Árbenz from government with backing from the U.S. Central Intelligence Agency. Castillo Armas strengthened political repression and undid many of the reforms after seizing office. His actions put an end to Guatemala's democratic experiment and contributed to the instability that followed for many years.

Cuba.  
  
I would also pick the 1950s as the most turbulent decade for Cuba. Growing popular discontent, armed uprisings, and ultimately a revolution that fundamentally altered the nation's political and economic structure all occurred during this period.

Fulgencio Batista, who took power in a military coup in 1952 and ruled Cuba as a dictator, was the first significant figure. His administration was infamous for its tight ties to powerful corporate interests, corruption, and censorship. Growing opposition was fueled by the perception among many Cubans that his administration disregarded the demands of common people.

Fidel Castro, who spearheaded a revolutionary movement against Batista's regime, was the second important person. Castro successfully started a guerilla war from the Sierra Maestra mountains after rebuilding his forces despite the failure of his initial attempts. Castro seized power in Cuba after Batista left the nation in 1959. His triumph signaled the start of a communist regime that fundamentally altered Cuban society and its ties to the US.

The political tensions of the Cold War had a significant impact on both Cuba and Guatemala, despite the fact that they underwent turmoil for different causes. Decades of violence followed the coup that put a stop to reform attempts in Guatemala. Widespread discontent with the regime in Cuba led to a successful revolution that changed the country. These significant turning points were largely shaped by the choices and leadership of Fulgencio Batista and Fidel Castro in Cuba, as well as Jacobo Árbenz and Carlos Castillo Armas in Guatemala.
