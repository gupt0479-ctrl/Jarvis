## What the OpenAI Student Collective is

The OpenAI Student Collective is a campus program built around a single role: the **Campus Lead**. Campus Leads are undergraduate students who bring AI programming to their own university — teaching peers to use ChatGPT and Codex, helping them start real projects, and putting that work in front of the wider campus.

OpenAI's framing is that students were among the first to find genuinely new uses for AI in learning, creativity, and problem-solving, and that students are best positioned to bring others along. The Collective is the structure that funds and supports that: Leads get the tools, training, funding, and backing to make it happen on their campus.

A defining feature is that **Campus Leads work in pairs**. OpenAI pairs each Lead with a complementary co-lead, on the reasoning that campus programming works best when two people can split the load and reach different parts of campus.

## Why the Campus Lead role matters for your career

It is easy to lump this in with the dozens of "brand ambassador" programs that give you a discount code and a t-shirt. This is a different category, for a few concrete reasons.

- **It is a paid, selective role at the most recognizable AI company in the world.** "OpenAI Campus Lead, OpenAI Student Collective" is a line a recruiter stops on. Selectivity plus a real stipend is what separates a credential from a participation badge.
- **It generates evidence, not just a title.** You will run four workshops a semester, weekly Studio Hours, and a showcase. That produces attendance numbers, project counts, and outcomes — exactly the quantified bullets that survive a résumé screen.
- **You get direct access to the OpenAI team** and a global network of Campus Leads. For students trying to break into AI, product, or research, that network is worth more than the stipend.
- **You build genuine tool fluency early.** A ChatGPT subscription, Codex credits, and first-party training mean you are learning the tooling ahead of your graduating class, not catching up to it.
- **It is leadership experience with a clear scope.** Interviewers love it because it answers "tell me about a time you built something from nothing" without you having to reach.

One practical note: the role explicitly excludes holding another ambassador position at the same time. Treat that as a signal of how seriously OpenAI takes it — and factor it into your decision if you already hold a similar role.

## How the program works

Campus Leads run four recurring formats. Understanding them before you apply matters, because your application should show you can actually execute them.

- **Workshops — four per semester.** Welcoming events where students learn new AI skills, explore what they can make, and pick a project to keep building. OpenAI supplies workshop guides, templates, and training; you adapt them to your campus. Most run one to two hours.
- **Studio Hours — weekly.** Coworking sessions where students put focused time into their projects, work alongside peers, and get help when they are stuck. This is where workshop curiosity turns into finished work.
- **Showcases — once each semester.** Campus-wide celebrations of what students built. This is your visible output and the thing that recruits next semester's attendees.
- **Community — ongoing.** A shared space where students stay connected, ask questions, and hear about upcoming opportunities.

Events are explicitly _not_ just for CS majors. OpenAI wants Leads who make sessions accessible to students across every discipline and experience level, including complete beginners. If your application only speaks to technical students, that is a mismatch with what they are looking for.

## What you receive as a Campus Lead

- A **ChatGPT subscription and Codex credits** for the duration of the program
- **Funding, credits, and merch** to organize your campus events
- **Training, resources, and guidance** on the latest OpenAI tools and technology
- **Access to the OpenAI team** and a global network of Campus Leads
- A **cash stipend each semester** in recognition of your time and leadership
- An opportunity for an **HQ visit in June 2027**, plus other perks along the way

Stipends are paid through OpenAI's payment platform at the end of each semester, after you have participated and completed the expected semester activities. Payment details are shared during onboarding.

## Eligibility checklist

Work through this before you spend time on the application. Every item is a hard requirement.

- You genuinely use and like ChatGPT, Codex, and OpenAI tools
- You are 18 years or older
- You are enrolled in an undergraduate college or university program as of August 2026 and expect to graduate **after December 2027**
- You can commit 4–6 hours per week on average from August 2026 through June 2027
- You study in the **United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, India, Japan, or South Korea** and have work authorization in your country of study
- You are not currently an ambassador or intern for any other AI program or company

Studying somewhere else? OpenAI still invites you to submit an application to register interest, and will notify you when the Collective becomes available in your country.

## The application, field by field

The form is short on paper and heavier than it looks in practice. Here is what it asks so you can prepare before you open it.

- **Basics** — first and last name as shown on your ID, email, and age
- **School details** — country of your university, institution name, educational status (undergraduate only), and expected graduation date
- **Field of study** — a broad category (Arts & Humanities, Business & Economics, Computer Science & Engineering, Design & Media, Law & Public Policy, Medicine & Healthcare, Natural Sciences & Research, Social Sciences, Other) plus your specific major in 50 characters or fewer
- **Commitment confirmation** — whether you can commit 4–6 hours per week from August 2026 through June 2027
- **Conflicts** — whether you currently hold a formal role with another company or program, including intern, fellow, ambassador, campus representative, student advisor, researcher, contractor, or formal volunteer
- **Tool usage** — which OpenAI tools you use (ChatGPT, Codex, the API, other, or none)
- **Two 60-second video responses** — covered in detail below
- **Two optional written questions** — the most important problem facing young people and how you would make progress on it, and something you have changed your mind about recently
- **Links** — LinkedIn profile and other public social profiles
- **Co-lead** — if you want to apply alongside a classmate, list the email they applied with. You each submit your own application and name one another.
- **History and source** — prior OpenAI program involvement (ChatGPT Lab, Codex Ambassador, OpenAI internship, other, or no) and how you heard about the role
- **Anything else** — a final 150-character free field

## How to make your two 60-second videos stand out

The videos are where this application is won or lost. Both are capped at 60 seconds and both are submitted as links, so **set sharing to "anyone with the link can view"** — OpenAI states explicitly that they cannot review videos requiring access. That single setting silently disqualifies applicants every cycle.

### Video 1: "What's one way you use AI that you think more students should know about?"

Do not describe ChatGPT generically. Show one specific, non-obvious workflow you personally use, ideally on screen. A structure that works in 60 seconds:

- **0:00–0:10** — name the problem students have ("everyone reads their lecture notes wrong")
- **0:10–0:40** — show the workflow, on screen if possible, in concrete steps
- **0:40–0:60** — the result, and why it generalizes to students in other majors

Strong answers usually come from outside CS: a nursing student building study simulations, a design student running critique loops, a debate student stress-testing arguments. Breadth is the point of the program.

### Video 2: "What's something you helped make happen on campus?"

This is the initiative test. They want evidence you can start something and get other people to show up. Answer it like a story with numbers:

- What did not exist before you
- What you specifically did — recruiting, booking, promoting, teaching
- The outcome, quantified: attendance, growth, money raised, projects shipped
- One honest thing that went wrong and what you changed

It does not need to be a large event. A 12-person reading group you started and kept alive for two semesters beats a 300-person event you volunteered at.

### Production notes

- Talk to the camera. Do not read a script word for word — it is obvious and it kills warmth.
- Quiet room, decent light on your face, phone camera is fine.
- Rehearse the timing so you finish inside 60 seconds without rushing the ending.
- Upload unlisted to YouTube or Google Drive, then open the link in a private window to verify access.

## Answering the written questions well

The two written questions are optional in the form but effectively not optional if you want to be competitive.

**"What is the most important problem facing young people? How would you try to make progress on it?"** Pick a narrow problem you have actually observed, not a headline. Then give a plan you could personally start next month. Specific and small beats sweeping and vague — they are testing whether you turn ideas into action.

**"What's something you've changed your mind about recently?"** This is an intellectual-honesty test. Name a real prior belief, the evidence or experience that moved you, and where you landed. Avoid humblebrags disguised as reversals.

## Preparing for the interview

OpenAI says interviews happen in August and are designed to learn about your **motivation, initiative, and community mindset**. Prepare one strong answer for each:

- **Motivation** — why AI on your campus specifically, and what you want students there to be able to do by June 2027.
- **Initiative** — a second story, different from your video, where you started something without being asked.
- **Community mindset** — how you would get a first-generation humanities student who is skeptical of AI to attend a second workshop.

Come with a rough semester plan: four workshop topics, where Studio Hours would happen, which campus groups you would partner with, and how you would promote the first event. Being the applicant who already has a plan is disproportionately effective.

## If you are not eligible or not selected

- **Outside the eight launch countries?** Submit the application anyway to register interest — OpenAI says they will notify you when the Collective reaches your country.
- **Graduate student?** You cannot be a Campus Lead, but you can attend campus events and workshops, and you can help your campus Lead run them.
- **Not selected?** Run the programming anyway. Start a small AI build night, keep attendance records, and reapply with real numbers. That is a stronger application than any essay.
- **Look at adjacent programs** — the ChatGPT Lab, Codex Ambassadors, and campus AI clubs all provide similar leadership evidence.

## FAQ

### What is the OpenAI Student Collective?

The OpenAI Student Collective is a program that selects undergraduate Campus Leads to run AI workshops, weekly Studio Hours, and end-of-semester showcases on their own campus. Leads work in pairs and receive training, funding, credits, merch, and a cash stipend each semester.

### When is the OpenAI Student Collective application deadline?

Applications are open through August 10, 2026 at 11:59pm PT. Selected applicants are invited to interview in August, and accepted Campus Leads onboard before launching campus programming in the fall.

### Is the OpenAI Campus Lead role paid?

Yes. Campus Leads receive a cash stipend each semester, paid at the end of the semester after completing the expected program activities, plus event funding, a ChatGPT subscription, and Codex credits.

### Do I need coding or Codex experience to apply?

No. OpenAI states you do not need Codex experience or a portfolio of AI projects. They look for curiosity, eagerness to learn, and the ability to bring people together, and they provide training before you introduce tools on campus.

### Do I need to start an official club to become a Campus Lead?

No. You can apply and begin hosting events without an officially recognized campus club. OpenAI provides resources, programming ideas, and funding, and can support you if official recognition would help you book rooms or reach students.

### Can graduate students apply to the OpenAI Student Collective?

No. The Campus Lead role is currently open only to undergraduate students who expect to graduate after December 2027. Postgraduate students are welcome to attend campus events and workshops.

### Can I hold another ambassador role at the same time?

No. Campus Leads cannot participate in another ambassador program while serving in the role. Campus clubs, student organizations, research groups, and internships are fine as long as they do not create a conflict or prevent you from meeting the commitment.
