# My Rights — The Standard at Dinkytown Lease

Minnesota law overrides several of the landlord-favorable clauses in the base contract. The **State Specific Addendum (Minnesota)** is where most of your real protections live — it explicitly says its terms "modify and supersede" the base Housing Contract. Read the base contract's Section 17 (entry) and Section 15 (security deposit) as replaced by what's below, not as the final word.

## Entry to your unit

The base contract says Landlord can enter "without notice" at reasonable hours. Minnesota law overrides this: Landlord must make a good-faith effort to give you **at least 24 hours' notice** before entering, except in genuine emergencies (safety, security, law enforcement). If they enter while you're out, they must leave written notice somewhere visible.

## Move-in and move-out inspections

- You can request a **move-in inspection** within 14 days of occupying, to document existing damage before it becomes "your" damage.
- You can request a **move-out inspection**, and you're entitled to be present for it. This is your chance to fix small things before they're deducted from your deposit. If you skip this, Landlord's obligation here is considered discharged — so it's worth doing.

## Security deposit

Your $100 deposit is held under Minnesota Statute 504B, earns **1% interest per year**, and must be returned — or itemized in a written statement explaining any withholding — within **21 days** of the latest of: your move-out date, returning your keys, or Landlord receiving your forwarding address.

## Right to call for help

You will **not** be evicted or penalized for calling the police during a domestic incident.

## Non-discrimination

Landlord cannot discriminate based on race, color, creed, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, disability, public assistance status, sexual orientation, or familial status.

## Right to information

Minnesota law requires Landlord to tell you that you can contact the state Attorney General with landlord-tenant questions: Office of Minnesota Attorney General, 445 Minnesota Street, Suite 1400, St. Paul, MN 55101, (651) 296-3353 / 1-800-657-3787.

## Rights that can't be waived

The contract cannot make you give up your right to bring an emergency resident-remedies action (MN Statute 504B.381) or a standard resident-remedies action (504B.395), no matter what other language is in the lease.

## Arbitration — you can opt out

The lease requires arbitration and waives your right to a jury trial or class action by default. You can decline this specific clause by mailing a written opt-out — your name, address, phone number, and a clear statement you're opting out — to Landmark Properties, 315 Oconee Street, Athens, GA 30601, **within 30 days** of becoming bound by the contract. Facsimile, PDF, and electronic signatures are treated as fully binding — same legal weight as an original wet signature, for both you and Landlord.
