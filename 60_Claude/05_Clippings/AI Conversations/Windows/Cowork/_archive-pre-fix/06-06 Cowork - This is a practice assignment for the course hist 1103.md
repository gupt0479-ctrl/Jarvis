Event: Nigerian Civil War (Biafran War), May 1967 – January 1970
Person: Odumegwu Ojukwu (Igbo military governor who declared Biafra independent)

Key data:
- Nigeria inherited colonial borders that merged Hausa-Fulani (north), Yoruba (west),
  and Igbo (southeast) — incompatible ethnic groups with no pre-colonial unity
- January 1966 coup (mostly Igbo officers) → July 1966 counter-coup (mostly northern)
  → pogroms killed ~30,000 Igbo in the north
- Ojukwu declared Republic of Biafra independent May 30, 1967; oil fields in Igbo territory
  were the core economic motivation for federal government to fight
- Federal blockade caused mass starvation; 1–3 million dead, majority civilian/children
- Images of starving Biafran children became the first major televised humanitarian crisis,
  directly creating the modern international humanitarian NGO movement (MSF founded 1971
  partly in response by French doctors who served in Biafra)
- Post-war: "No victor, no vanquished" — but Igbo land and property not restored;
  ethnic tension in Nigeria persists through Boko Haram, IPOB separatists today

CARIBBEAN — Cuba / Turmoil type: Revolution

Event: Cuban Revolution, 1953–1959; Bay of Pigs 1961; Cuban Missile Crisis 1962
Person: Fidel Castro

Key data:
- Batista regime: US-backed, casino-era Havana; massive inequality
- Castro's 26th of July Movement launched July 26 1953 (Moncada Barracks attack, failed;
  Castro captured, tried, released)
- Guerrilla campaign from Sierra Maestra; Batista fled January 1 1959
- CIA-trained 1,400 Cuban exiles landed at Bay of Pigs April 17 1961; defeated in 3 days;
  Kennedy refused air cover; diplomatic catastrophe for the US
- October 1962: US U-2 aircraft photographed Soviet missile sites; 13-day standoff;
  Kennedy-Khrushchev back-channel deal (Soviets remove missiles; US pledges not to invade Cuba)
- US embargo imposed 1962, still in place; Cuba remained communist through Soviet collapse
- Regional export of revolution: 20,000 Cuban troops in Angola by 1975 (backed MPLA);
  Cuban advisors trained Sandinistas in Nicaragua; Che Guevara killed in Bolivia 1967
  attempting to replicate Cuba elsewhere

SOUTH AMERICA — Chile / Turmoil type: Foreign military involvement

Event: CIA-backed military coup, September 11 1973; Pinochet dictatorship 1973–1990
Person: Augusto Pinochet / Salvador Allende

Key data:
- Allende elected 1970 — first democratically elected Marxist head of state in history;
  immediately nationalized copper industry (Anaconda, Kennecott — US corporations)
- Nixon: "Make the economy scream"; CIA spent $8 million destabilizing Chile 1970-73
- September 11 1973: military jets bombed La Moneda presidential palace; Allende died
  (suicide or killed — disputed); Pinochet assumed power that day
- Operation Condor: US-coordinated network linking Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay,
  Bolivia, Brazil — sharing intelligence and extraditing/disappearing political opponents
  across borders; hundreds killed in third countries
- 3,000+ executed or disappeared under Pinochet; 30,000 tortured; Villa Grimaldi
  detention center documented; 200,000 exiled
- "Chicago Boys": University of Chicago-trained economists implemented radical free-market
  shock therapy in Chile — became the neoliberal template exported across Latin America
  in the 1980s-90s (Bolivia, Argentina, Peru followed similar programs under IMF pressure)
- Pinochet arrested in London 1998 on Spanish warrant; international precedent for
  universal jurisdiction in human rights cases
