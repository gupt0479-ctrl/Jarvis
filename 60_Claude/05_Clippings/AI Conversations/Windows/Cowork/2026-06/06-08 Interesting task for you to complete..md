## Question 1 — DevoreStat9 1.1.003 (Warranty service)

**(a) Probability questions about a sample of 900:**

✅ **What is the expected number among the 900 that need warranty service?**
✅ **How likely is it that more than half of the sampled computers will need or have needed warranty service?**
✅ **How likely is it that the number needing warranty service will exceed the expected number by more than 10?**

❌ *What is the number of computers that will need warranty service in the population?* — This asks about the full population, not a probabilistic question about the sample.
❌ *How many more than half the population will need warranty service?* — Same issue; it's about the population, not the sample.

**(b) Inferential statistics questions answered by the sample:**

✅ **Does the sample provide compelling evidence for concluding that more than 10% of all such computers need warranty service?**
✅ **Suppose that 135 of the 900 sampled needed warranty service. How confident can we be that the proportion of *all such computers* needing warranty service is between 0.08 and 0.22?**

❌ *Does the population provide compelling evidence about the sample?* — Backwards; inference goes sample → population, not the other way.
❌ *How confident can we be that the proportion of sampled computers is between 8 and 22?* — The sample proportion is already known (135/900 ≈ 15%); there's nothing to infer about the sample itself.
❌ *Can we conclude the population will have the same percentage as the sample?* — Not a valid inferential statement; samples estimate population parameters with uncertainty.

---

## Question 2 — DevoreStat9 1.1.005 (Supplemental Instruction)

**(a) Are the SI group scores from an existing population?**

**No.** → **They are from the conceptual population of all students taking a large statistics course who participate in an SI program of this sort.**

There is no pre-existing group of students who went through this specific SI program — the treatment group is part of a new experiment, so the relevant population is conceptual (hypothetical).

**(b) Primary advantage of random division:**

✅ **The two groups should be fairly comparable before the study.**

Random assignment balances both known and unknown confounding variables (e.g., prior math ability, motivation) between groups, so any difference in outcomes can be attributed to SI participation.

**(c) Why not put all students in the treatment group:**

✅ **To have some results to compare to each other.**

Without a control group, you have no baseline. You couldn't know whether SI improved scores or whether those students would have performed the same way regardless.

---

## Question 3 — CSU sampling methods

✅ **The sample could be generated by taking a stratified random sample — a simple random sample from each of the 23 campuses.**
✅ **Certain problems arise with self-reporting of distances, such as recording error or poor recall.**
✅ **One could take a simple random sample of students from all students in the CSU system.**

❌ *There are no potential problems with self-reporting of distances.* — False; self-reporting is subject to recall bias, rounding, and recording errors.
❌ *Instead of taking a random sample, every student should be included.* — A census of all CSU students is impractical and unnecessary to make a reliable inference.

---

## Question 4 — Real estate appraiser

✅ **An appropriate method is to generate a simple random sample of all single-family homes in the city, or a stratified random sample by taking a simple random sample from each of the ten district neighborhoods.**

The stratified approach is especially sensible here because home values vary systematically by neighborhood — stratifying ensures representation from each district. The other options (newest homes, most expensive homes, one randomly selected district) all introduce systematic bias.

---

## Question 5 — Michelson and Newcomb light-travel measurements

**(a) Why are the 66 measurements not identical?**

✅ **Differences in weather conditions at time of measurement** — atmospheric refraction and air density affect the apparent travel time.
✅ **Measurement error** — inherent imprecision in any physical instrument.
✅ **Recording error** — human error when writing down readings.

❌ *Variances in the speed of light* — the speed of light is a physical constant; it does not vary.
❌ *Light error* — not a meaningful scientific category.
